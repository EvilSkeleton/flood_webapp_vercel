60 months, four gates. No gate, no next tranche.

  1. G1
    End of M6Data agreements signed

    End of M1480% accuracy in shadow mode
  3. G3
    End of M24Build accepted

Shadow mode: the model runs on live data and its predictions are checked against what actually happens, but no public alerts are issued from it.

09 · Risks and answers

What could go wrong, and what we do about it.

Four questions the Department of Expenditure will ask

What if the model is less accurate?
It still breaks even at about 56% accuracy. Gate G2 stops the rollout if shadow mode does not reach 80%.
What if it costs more?
Even the cautious case absorbs a 43% overrun. A 5% contingency is already included.
How will we know it worked?
Months 37 to 60 measure lead time, floods caught, false alarms and losses against the 2015–2024 baseline.
Who is answerable if a warning is missed?
The three-line rule. Every alert, review and decision is logged against a named officer.

    Trained on the synthetic sample dataset supplied with the case (Flood_Prediction_v2.1). Outcome fields (water depth, NDWI, risk score, severity) were excluded to prevent leakage. The threshold is set at 30% because a missed flood costs lives and a false alarm costs trust. At 30%, cross-validated recall is about 95% with about 84% precision; at 40% it would be 92% and 89%. Brahmaputra alone has only 85 rows, so its recall is noisy. Field accuracy will be lower than on synthetic data; this is a prototype, not an operational warning system.
